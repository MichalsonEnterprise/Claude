## SECTION 02 — WHY IP MATTERS IN VENTURE

**Eyebrow:**
THE GAP

**Headline:**
The most expensive diligence question is the least asked.

**Body:**
Early-stage diligence interrogates markets, teams, and traction — and routinely takes "proprietary technology" on faith. Yet defensibility failures are among the most terminal outcomes a startup can have: blocking prior art discovered post-investment, IP owned by a former employer or university, a landscape crowded with patents the deck never mentioned.

At the stage where technology is most of the valuation, IP analysis isn't a specialist add-on. It's the difference between examining a company and examining its story.

## SECTION 04 — WHAT WE EXAMINE

**Eyebrow:**
THE EXAMINATION

**Headline:**
Six questions every technology company gets asked.

**Examination list:**
1. **Defensibility** — Is the core advantage protectable — by patent, secret, or structural moat — and is the protection real today?
2. **Prior art** — Does earlier art exist that undermines novelty? What survives contact with the search?
3. **IP ownership** — Are assignments complete? Any founder, employer, or university claims outstanding?
4. **Competitive landscape** — Who holds patents nearby? Where is freedom to operate genuinely open, and where is it contested?
5. **Evidence** — Can the company prove when its work existed? Are records timestamped and retrievable?
6. **Technology differentiation** — Beyond legal protection: is the technical approach meaningfully distinct, or is the difference marketing?
